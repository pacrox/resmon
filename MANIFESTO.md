
# PROTEST MANIFESTO: PRIVACY, DIGITAL FREEDOM, AND POLITICAL BETRAYAL

*This is a symbolic, non-binding statement of protest. It does not alter the
terms of the MIT License under which this software is distributed — see
LICENSE.txt.*

This software is licensed under the terms of the MIT License and is technically free to use, modify, and distribute worldwide. The author states, as a symbolic act of protest, that they do not wish it to be deployed, executed, hosted, or used within **Brazil, the State of California, and the State of Colorado**.

This is a symbolic protest against the local *Age Verification Laws* (such as Brazil's ECA Digital and similar US state mandates). By forcing software developers and operating systems to act as identity gatekeepers, these regulations threaten privacy, destroy digital autonomy, and legally enforce a corporate monopoly over everyday technology.



This short-sighted political calculation will not appease those who seek absolute control. 
Instead, it merely alienates and disillusions the very progressive electorate that built this administration, while dangerous normalization is given to an authoritarian surveillance agenda. 
By handing over these turnkey tracking tools, the current leadership is tragically clearing the path for its most fierce adversaries to inherit a pre-built panopticon. 

When the pendulum of power inevitably swings back, these exact legal mechanisms will be left 
in the hands of forces eager to revive the darkest chapters of the nation's past. They will possess the absolute digital leverage needed to silence dissent, enforce future repressions, and freely exploit the country’s natural sanctuaries and the Amazon forest without resistance. 
What was meant to be a historic legacy has instead become a blueprint for capitulation—a bitter compromise that history, and the true giants of past social struggles, will judge severely.

**If you live in these regions, please join the fight to protect digital freedom, or use proprietary tools that comply with your governments' corporate surveillance standards.**
